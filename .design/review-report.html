Wake admin redesign contest

20 November 2026 · Design decision

Choose Entry 1, Source Reader, as the foundation. It gives Wake its clearest answer to “Where did this number come from?” A reader can follow an exact price to the broker’s entry, see what Wake accepted, and return to the same place. Borrow Entry 2’s compact Overview and trade details, then use Entry 3’s language and per-stream status for synchronization.

The same account, three opening screens

All three builds had one MSFT disagreement, a confirmation received on 3 November, and July reporting dates. Source Reader immediately names the affected trade and separates the latest receipt from the date of the positions. Its Synchronization heading starts 1,199 px down the desktop page. Entry 2 puts the review and latest report side by side, with Synchronization at 471 px. Entry 3 puts Synchronization at 147 px and shows the last retrieval for each stream. At 390 px wide, its Financial review heading begins 766 px down an 844 px screen.

The same fill, three ways to find it

The original confirmation contains two MSFT buys at 400.123456789 USD. I opened the first, broker trade 900000001, from the report in each build. All three records preserved the full price and the quantity 0.125000000000 shares. The difference was what I could know before opening the record, and what happened when I went back.

  • Source Reader gave each fill a price and a distinct source ID. I could read the selected broker row beside the neighboring rows, open the record, then follow View price source back to the entry. The grid’s “Source ID” is the execution identifier. The broker trade ID appears in the original XML. Naming both would help someone working from a broker statement.
  • Financial statement put the broker trade ID under Buy MSFT in the row itself. This was the fastest way to choose between the two buys when I already knew the ID. On the trade, Source reports explains what each appearance contributed. Back restored the record link.
  • Constrained polish showed “Open activity”, the XML location, and a rule version. To select the same fill I had to know it was TradeConfirm[1]. Back reopened the report with focus on the document body at both tested widths. Give the link a trade name and ID, and restore focus to that link when the reader returns.

The mobile captures show the point immediately before opening the record. Entry 1 has an intermediate reading view. Entries 2 and 3 open the record directly from the table.

Taste visual critique

Source Reader wins on visuals, followed by Financial statement and Constrained polish. Entry 2 has the best everyday Overview. I used Taste’s redesign criteria for typography, color, spacing, visual hierarchy, and continuity with the existing brand. For this interface the test is whether the composition helps someone read exact financial evidence several times a day.

1 · Source Reader. The evergreen masthead leaves the width of a report available for reading. STIX Two Text gives the two prices equal typographic weight, and the sage background and “Accepted price” label distinguish Wake’s decision. On a phone the prices stack in reading order with their full precision intact. The large tinted assessment at the top of Overview competes with this strength. Give a quiet account a compact status line and reserve the larger treatment for a decision with an immediate action. Keep the paired prices and ruled report pages.

2 · Financial statement. The deep-green rail, ivory page, serif headings, and thin rules give the desktop trade the rhythm of a statement. Face amount, price per 100, and reported principal each have a clear place across a single line. The same restraint works on Overview. On the narrow report, however, the first columns consume the screen before the effect on the record appears. Use two lines per mobile entry. Put the trade and its ID first, then the exact terms and effect. Keep the full table on desktop.

3 · Constrained polish. The steel surfaces, copper links, and familiar wordmark preserve the incumbent identity. The mixed-stream view makes good use of repeated alignment. An operator can compare a failed request, an ordinary retry, and a healthy stream without learning a new layout. Overview gives the bordered sync panel and its button most of the visual weight even when all streams are idle. Keep its plain labels and alignment, move each repair beside its blocker, and let the outstanding decision take the strongest emphasis. Move XML paths and rule versions into an expandable row detail.

What to carry into the next build

  1. Start with Entry 1’s report reader. Keep Reports in primary navigation and preserve the selected report entry, original terms, current value, and return position. Fix report search first. Entering the displayed title “Trade confirmations” returned zero results in the matched build. Keep the two exact-price fills and the saved 450 USD → 402.0000 USD correction as acceptance cases. Extend paired comparisons to other fields only where Wake can establish which source or decision supplied the value.
  2. Use Entry 2’s Overview composition and trade summary. Lead with the affected record and the latest report, then show per-stream status. Keep the summary of both appearances of bill 900000017. Show 10000 nominal, 99.1234567 USD per 100, and 9912.35 USD reported principal on the report as well as the trade. The submitted report row drops “per 100”.
  3. Use Entry 3’s sync language and honest source links. Show last successful retrieval, next attempt or retry, and a nearby repair action. Offer Request sync when it can advance work. Keep Recent reports from this source on Positions when Wake only knows the connection. Give a report awaiting processing its own message. A zero-row result currently produces “No activity entries in this report”, which can overstate what Wake knows.
  4. Validate the combined journey before adoption. Use two like-priced fills, an overlapping report, an unlinked entry, a saved correction, and a source that cannot sync. Check the original terms, the accepted value, and keyboard focus on return at 390 px and 200% zoom. Time report opening and reverse lookup on a large synthetic history. Entry 1 still sends the whole retained report in its closed disclosure, and Entry 2’s reverse lookup scans retained appearances. The small fixtures establish behavior but give no production latency estimate.

I recommend GPT-6.2 Sol with high reasoning for the report-to-record integration and medium reasoning for bounded layout, wording, and browser verification.

Evidence and limits

The comparison used the three staged submissions on the same base, 37854bc. I built each staged tree in isolation and ran the same synthetic acquisition and source-conflict sequence. I used Chromium through Playwright at 1360 × 900 light and 390 × 844 dark, with reduced motion enabled. In all six cases I activated the record link by keyboard, inspected the same MSFT fill, and used browser Back. The two comparison figures come from those runs.

The correction and Treasury bill figures come from the entrants’ synthetic walkthroughs. The mixed-sync figure comes from a fresh Chromium fixture run. Focused Go checks passed for Entries 1 and 2, and focused Chromium checks passed for Entry 3. Entrant logs record full Go and Chromium passes for Entries 1 and 2. Entry 3 completed Chromium and focused Go checks, and stopped its full Go run before completion. Screen-reader use, operator preference, and performance on large histories remain unmeasured.

Useful source locations in the respective staged worktrees include Entry 1’s internal/transport/admin/source_evidence.go and internal/persistence/sqlite/sql/admin_queries.sql, Entry 2’s internal/transport/admin/grids.go and readmodels.go in the same directory, and Entry 3’s internal/transport/admin/workspaces.templ, grids.go, and assets/app.js.
